# Contribution emphasis without a contribution list

Source: the per-paper reading notes on how each of the six house papers H1-H6 states its contribution (not shipped); wording checked with `tools/ps.py -q` and `assets/lexicon/lexicon.md`. "Method papers" = H1, H4, H5, H6; "findings papers" = H2, H3; "n/6" = how many of the six house papers do it. The house papers' own method names are replaced by X and the prior methods they build on by <Prior>. The Introduction's argument chain is in `introduction.md`; this file covers how the contribution is made visible everywhere else (abstract, teaser, method opener, experiments, limitations, conclusion, Related Works).

## A.0 In one sentence

None of the six finals has a contribution list, yet each contribution is clear, because of a fixed kit: **one name + one fixed two-word epithet (or core term) + one pair of axes**, repeated verbatim in the same order in the abstract, Introduction, Methodology opener, Experiments and Conclusion; **the drawback labels come back unchanged as the things the experiments test**; **the teaser carries the contrast**; novelty is read by the reader from a contrast with a named assumption or practice, never claimed as novel / first; verbs are tiered by the strength of the evidence, numbers appear only in the evidence layer, the conclusion is the abstract compressed into the past tense, and the limitations state only scope and one cost.

## A.2 Verb tiers (by evidence strength)

| Tier | Verbs / qualifiers | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | may / can / risk / potentially / "may hinder … in practice" | H5 "two key limitations that may hinder the memory stability and efficiency of reasoning in practice"; H1 "potentially impede"; H6 "which may lead to suboptimal reasoning" |
| Method | propose (1-3 times per paper: abstract / intro / conclusion), introduce, present, augment, equip, incorporate | H5 "The key idea is to augment the recurrent workflow with two text-controlled gates"; H4 "we present"; H1's "propose" appears once, in the abstract; H2 and H3 finals use "propose" 0 times |
| Result | show / validate / verify / demonstrate, with a scope | H5 "we show that X generally outperforms", "we validate the effectiveness of the two introduced gating mechanisms"; H4 "demonstrate the effectiveness" |
| Interpretation | suggest / indicate / may / possible / attribute | H2 "This outcome suggests the possible homomorphism …, implying that collaborative signals may be implicitly encoded" (four hedges in one sentence) |
| Math | equivalent / special case / ensure / guarantee, with ≈ / nearly in the object | H1 theorem "**equivalent**" (the only bold claim in the paper); H4 "ensures that $\Delta h_b\approx 0$", "guaranteeing … nearly unaffected" |
| Stance | advocate / believe (H1 only, retired) | H1 "we advocate its adoption as a standard loss"; the lexicon marks it retired |

## A.3 Conclusion, limitations, numbers, "outperform"

- **Conclusion**: 3-6 sentences, past tense, recovering the abstract's epithet verbatim (H4, H1, H2); the evidence sentence keeps only the main result, not the analysis findings (H5 does not mention the gate analysis, H3 dropped C4); the closing value sentence at most "can help the community better understand …" (H3).
- **Limitations**: only "not covered" plus one cost, and the cost sits next to your own core word (H5 sells memory stability and admits reduced training stability); a concessive opener is optional (H4 "Despite showing the effectiveness of X"; H5's final version deleted "Despite demonstrating stability and efficiency").
- **Numbers**: only in the evidence layer, attributed to the design that produces them, written as "range + metric + reference" (H1 "improves Recall@20 by 10.00% to 21.90% compared to <base loss> and by 5.75% to 10.90% compared to the second-best baseline").
- **outperform**: check the table cell by cell; one lost cell means no "all / consistently"; the qualifier is the same in the abstract, intro, bold sentences and conclusion.

## C.7 Stating contributions while results are pending

The six papers all had results, but the tiering rules extend directly to a paper whose experiments are not run yet:

1. **The epithet must be a property that holds by construction.** H4's "empirically effective" needs experiments; an epithet like "single-pass and budget-aware" (the data are read once; the budget is set after training) is guaranteed by the design, so it can be repeated in all four places now without overclaiming. **Do not** coin effective / accurate / faithful epithets that need results.
2. **Design facts in the present tense, attributed to the design that produces them.** Cost bookkeeping, "any setting evaluated with forward passes only", a shared control run are construction facts, like H5's abstract mechanism sentence "the memory only updates when the update gate is open".
3. **Theory as "we show" + premise + exact coefficient** (math tier), on a par with H1's bold "equivalent" and H4's "ensure … ≈"; anything under an approximate model is "remains a hypothesis".
4. **Honest reservations become RQ premises**, never a paragraph's or the abstract's closer ("Since, to first order, …, we investigate whether …"; "Therefore, we include … as baselines").
5. **The evaluation design is itself a contribution, but its verbs stay at the design tier**: design / investigate whether / is expected to; never verify / validate / demonstrate / "show that X matches". Criteria fixed in advance + rejection conditions are a stronger form of H3's "then the four vectors are expected to show …".
6. **No result sentence in the conclusion**: past-tense proposed (method), showed (theory), designed (evaluation); the evaluation sentence says what is compared in RQ1 → RQ2 order, and does not stake the paper on a comparison you predict to tie.
7. **Limitation tense matches the evidence state**: "leaving X to the experiments in Section …", never "are tested".
8. **When results arrive**: the abstract's last sentence may become "Experiments with … show that …" (with generally / on most pairs / mainly on small-gap pairs, checked cell by cell), the conclusion gains one evidence sentence, and a counter-example goes into the limitations as a cost (H5 style); the epithet and component names do not change.

## E. Rules with checks (KR1-KR14)

`scripts/check_paper.py` automates the greppable parts in its `== intro & contributions` block (declare the epithet with a `% epithet: <phrase>` line in the main file, next to the `% concept:` table).

1. **KR1 No contribution list; no "novel" / "first".** In the abstract, Introduction and Conclusion: 0 "novel", 0 self-referential "first", 0 "for the first time", 0 "To the best of our knowledge"; "first" at most once, in Related Works, right next to the work it goes beyond. *Check*: grep, excluding ordinals and first-order.
2. **KR2 One epithet, four places, verbatim.** Abstract proposal sentence, intro proposal sentence (optionally also the standard sentence and the "We highlight" sentence), Methodology opener, Conclusion method sentence; the epithet never changes, only the tense. After any rewrite or plain-terms pass, re-check that the epithet survived (one draft's abstract lost it in a plain-terms polishing pass). While results are pending, only a property that holds by construction (no effective / accurate). *Check*: `% epithet:` + `check_paper.py`, all four places hit.
3. **KR3 The epithet's halves = the negation of the two drawback labels; no third pair on the problem side.** *Check*: list "drawback label → epithet word"; grep problem-side adjective pairs (e.g. costly / inflexible) and rewrite them as the epithet's negation ("no existing <proxy> is A or B").
4. **KR4 Component names used unchanged, and as section titles.** Named once in the intro with "(termed …)"; method subsection titles "<component name> with <tool>"; RQ labels and experiment subsection titles use the same component names; the roadmap sentence repeats the subsection titles verbatim. *Check*: each component name in the intro, method title, roadmap, RQ label and conclusion, with no synonym.
5. **KR5 Drawback labels are the test objects.** Each drawback label (or its fixed phrase) appears verbatim in: the method subsection opener ("To address the <label>"), at least one RQ opener or prediction, the conclusion's first sentence, one RW closer. *Check*: list the hits per label; fewer than 4 places → add.
6. **KR6 Novelty by contrast, with the scope stated exactly.** At least one sentence names the design all prior work shares (the root-cause sentence, `introduction.md` IR1), and the method sentence negates it with the same words; each technical difference gets a "rather than <prior practice>", and the contrast points only at the works that really do this (if it only holds for prior methods that use component Z, do not write "with or without Z"); the one technical difference is named in the abstract or intro. *Check*: root-cause sentence next to the key idea, antonym pairs marked; the citations of the contrast sentence match the RW grouping.
7. **KR7 Verbs by evidence tier; with results pending, stop at the design tier.** Problem may / can / risk; method propose / introduce / train; theory show / reduce (with premise); interpretation suggest / may / "remains a hypothesis"; prediction "is expected to". When tables still hold "--" or `\pend`, no demonstrate / validate / verify / prove / consistently / significantly for your own results, and no present or present-perfect tense claiming a test was done. *Check*: `check_paper.py` greps these words when pending cells are found; read each sentence's tense.
8. **KR8 Numbers only in the evidence layer, attributed to their design.** 0 result numbers in abstract and conclusion; the intro may hold only setting and bookkeeping numbers, each traceable to the design that produces it (a 10× saving from a shorter run, not from a shared component). *Check*: list every intro number with its source sentence.
9. **KR9 "outperform" bounded, one strength everywhere.** outperform / improve in intro, bold sentences and conclusion carry generally / on most … / mainly on …; cell-by-cell check; one lost cell means no all / consistently; the same qualifier in abstract, intro, bold sentence, conclusion. *Check*: a four-place qualifier table.
10. **KR10 Reservations as premises, never closers.** The last sentences of the abstract, the method paragraph and the intro contain no however / unclear / "whether … any benefit"; a result such as "equivalent to first order" becomes "Since …, we investigate whether …" or "Therefore, we include … as baselines". *Check*: read each paragraph's last sentence (`check_paper.py` flags intro and abstract paragraphs ending on however / unclear / any benefit).
11. **KR11 Conclusion = the abstract in the past tense.** 3-4 sentences: problem → method (epithet + component names) → theory / evidence → evaluation; past tense; same order as the abstract; no qualifier added or dropped (counter-example: H4 "nearly zero" → "zero"); with results pending, the last sentence says what is compared, in RQ order, not a comparison predicted to tie. *Check*: align abstract and conclusion sentence by sentence.
12. **KR12 Limitations = one cost + one scope, never in conflict with a slogan.** Before the Conclusion; "Several limitations remain." + "On the one hand, <cost>" + "On the other hand, <scope>, leaving … unknown / to …"; no remedies or promises; check every slogan or label in the paper (an RQ label "Efficiency", a "free-lunch") against the limitations; the limitation tense matches the evidence state. *Check*: slogan → limitation table.
13. **KR13 The teaser carries the contrast.** The caption states the conclusion with a pair of contrasting verbs ("<prior> re-train … in each run, whereas <ours> trains … once"), names each component once, and gives the honest cost range; one or two words on the figure mark the two axes. *Check*: the first two caption sentences hold the contrast and the component names; the figure text covers both axes.
14. **KR14 Credit to the mechanism; borrowed parts credited on the spot; no self-deprecation in RW.** Borrowed parts credited in the same sentence (following / as in / widely adopted); expected or actual gains attributed to a named mechanism ("This is because …", "We attribute …"); RW paragraphs end on a drawback label or "remains largely underexplored", and the last paragraph recovers everything with the epithet; never "is not our contribution" (as a closer), "this mapping is simple", "Our work differs from them". *Check*: read each RW closer; `check_paper.py` greps the phrases it can ("is not our contribution", "Our work differs", "Different from them"; self-deprecation such as "this mapping is simple" is not greppable, so read for it).

## Worked case: a contribution map of a draft with pending results

Mapping every contribution sentence of one unpublished method draft by place and strength (abstract, teaser caption, intro, method opener, RQs, limitations, conclusion, RW closers) gives a checklist of typical slips:
- **Overclaims** (each fixed by one rule): "replaces each <expensive evaluation> with two short runs" while the expensive evaluation still runs for the selected candidates (KR6); a cost saving attributed to the shared component instead of the shorter run (KR8); a theory result stated against "training directly on the combined data" when it compares runs of the same length (KR6); "are only tested empirically" while the result cells are empty (KR7, KR12); an RQ label "Efficiency" while the limitations admit the method is no cheaper than an equal-length baseline (KR12); a contrast "with or without Z" that only holds for methods using Z (KR6); a theory sentence missing its premise (`introduction.md` IR15).
- **Underclaims**: the epithet and the fixed component names missing from the abstract and the teaser caption (KR2, KR4, KR13); the only technical difference unnamed and uncontrasted in abstract and intro (KR6); the method paragraph and the abstract ending on "no difference at first order" (KR10); the conclusion staking the paper on the one comparison predicted to tie (KR11); "is not our contribution" and "this mapping is simple" in Related Works (KR14).
- **Done well**: borrowed parts credited in the same sentence (D15); predictions fixed in advance, with rejection conditions (D16, stronger than the house papers); limitations as "Several limitations remain." + On the one hand cost + On the other hand scope (D12); method subsection titles in the "P with <tool>" form (D4).
