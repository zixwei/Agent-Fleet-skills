# Introduction logic: the argument chain, prior-work moves, revision patterns

Source: the per-paper reading notes on the six house papers H1-H6 and on four reference papers (refA-1, refA-2 from the refA lineage; refB-1, refB-2 from the refB lineage; comparison only), which are not shipped with the skill. Wording checked with `tools/ps.py` and `assets/lexicon/lexicon.md`. "Method papers" = H1, H4, H5, H6; "findings papers" = H2, H3; "n/6" = how many of the six house papers do it (H1 is the oldest; H4-H6 are the current ones). Quotes from the reference papers are kept under 15 words; the house papers' own method names are replaced by X and the prior methods they build on by <Prior>.

How this file relates to the others: `prose.md` §3 gives the paragraph templates (what each Introduction paragraph contains) and §7 the Related Works arc; this file gives the **logic** (why each sentence follows from the one before, and where the method's words come from). Contribution emphasis (epithet, verb tiers, conclusion, limitations) is in `contributions.md`.

## 0. Write the chain before the prose

Before drafting any Introduction sentence, write the argument as 8-10 one-line claims in a `%` block at the top of the intro file (H3 and H5 outline as comments). Draft only when every link can be written; a link you cannot write is a gap in the argument, and polishing the prose will not hide it.

```
% CHAIN (one line per claim; the word in <> is reused verbatim later)
% 1  X decides Y, so practitioners repeatedly do Z.                          importance -> task (plant a premise / seed word)
% 2  While important, doing Z reliably is hard: (a) ..., (b) ....           each listed item is cashed later
% 3  The prevailing practice does Z by <mechanism, in our notation>; it works well (hedged).
%    Plant the weaknesses as neutral details: <detail-1> (a modifier, a step, a formula term), <detail-2>.
% 4  ROOT CAUSE: "Whether A or B, these methods share one design: ..."     neutral; names the shared assumption
% 5  STANDARD: "Intuitively, effective Z should be <P> (\ie ...) and <Q> (\ie ...)."   P, Q = the method epithet
% 6  (i) <detail-1> -> violates P -> consequence;  (ii) <detail-2> -> violates Q -> consequence.
%    The drawback wording pre-announces the method (the missing decision IS the method).
% 7  KEY IDEA = literal negation of the root cause (reuse >= 2 of its content words, flipped).
% 8  CHALLENGE: "However, <why the key idea alone is not enough> \cite{}." -> "We therefore <component C>."
% 9  Component 1 <-> (i), component 2 <-> (ii); "together, they make Z P and Q."
% 10 First ... (\cf Section). Second ... (\cf Section). Third .... "We highlight that X makes Z P and Q."
%    No contribution list.
```

Five checks on the chain (each is a rule in §6):
1. Line 4 exists and is neutral (no "fail", "wrong", "waste").
2. Line 5 comes before line 6, and its two words are the epithet of line 9 and of the abstract, method opener and conclusion (`contributions.md` KR2).
3. Every noun phrase in line 6 appears first in line 3 (the planted detail).
4. Line 7 and line 4 side by side show antonym pairs (all → each, fixed → dynamic, together → separate, update every step → decide whether to update).
5. Every modifier and component in lines 7-9 (a "short", a "weighted", a "gate") is motivated by an earlier line or by the challenge sentence in line 8.

## 2. Method papers vs findings papers

| Step | Method papers (H1, H4, H5, H6) | Findings papers (H2, H3) |
|---|---|---|
| Shape of the drawback | a design of current practice causes a measurable consequence (I7, 4/4) | a premise or mechanism "has not been studied": H2 S8 "explicit studies of the relationship between language and behavior spaces remain largely unexplored"; H3 S9 "the underlying nature and internal structure of these mechanisms remain largely unexplored" |
| Where importance hangs | on the consequence of the drawback (I15) | on the object of study, before the gap (H3 S3-S4); the ending recovers it with applications |
| From drawback to proposal | key idea → (challenge) → components → mapping sentence | research question → tool → **first say what a positive result would mean** (H2 S11 "Positive results would imply that ..."; H3 S17 "Positive findings would indicate that ...", 2/2) → teaser gives the answer |
| Ending | First/Second/Third evidence roadmap | finding bullets + potentials / applications |

A method paper **without results yet** follows the method chain but may borrow the findings papers' "Positive results would indicate that ..." as a reading fixed in advance in P5, instead of ending the method paragraph on an open doubt (§6 IR8; `contributions.md` C.7).

## 3. Why the reader feels "there was no other way"

1. **The criticism is read out of facts, not imposed**: the weakness already appeared as a neutral detail (I4) and the root cause was named first (I5).
2. **The ruler comes first**: the standard (I6) precedes the drawbacks, and drawbacks, method and evidence are measured with the same ruler (I13).
3. **The method is the drawbacks' list of antonyms**: swap the verbs and nouns of the drawback sentences for their opposites and you get the method (I8, I9); the components reuse the drawbacks' numbering (I17).
4. **No reason appears from nowhere**: every component that does not follow from the idea is preceded by a challenge or a premise (I11; H6 s19 puts the reason in a since-clause of the same sentence).
5. **Only one battle**: only fixable drawbacks (I14), at most one named object of modification (RW6), the closest competitors in a citation cluster or in Related Works.

## 5. Revision patterns across papers (draft → final)

Two or three pairs each; edits by senior co-authors quoted under 15 words.

| # | Pattern | Draft → final |
|---|---|---|
| RV1 | **Lower the claim strength** | H1 "the primary contributor to boosted performance" → "plays a more crucial role in boosting performance in CF than heuristic-based graph augmentation" (superlative → comparative); H1 "we identify and substantiate two key limitations" → "two critical limitations have been observed that potentially impede"; H2 "we propose a new ... paradigm" → "we explore the possibility of building advanced CF models", "for its originality" → "for its simplicity"; H5 "avoids memory explosion" → "mitigating memory explosion" |
| RV2 | **Evaluative adjective → causal verb chain** | H1 "is widely acknowledged as insufficient to provide reliable and high-quality contrastive signals" → "overlooks the risk of false negatives, thus failing to provide reliable contrastive signals"; H1 "contradicts real-world scenarios, thus ultimately leading to sub-optimal representation learning" → "this inherent inductive bias is rarely considered in current CL-based CF methods, resulting in suboptimal recommendation quality and generalization ability"; H3 "has attracted wide attention ..." → "As a result, it has emerged as a critical factor for both ..." |
| RV3 | **"aim to / try to" → say what it does** | H4 "Vector calibration aims to refine the refusal direction" → "Vector calibration refines the refusal direction"; H4 appendix "Recent works also try to introduce reasoning process" → "More recent studies further incorporate explicit reasoning processes"; H3 "try to extract" → "further extract", "we aim to find clues for these questions" → "We approach these questions from the perspective of model activations" |

| RV11 | **One idea per sentence; split long ones and join with a shell noun** | H5: a ~45-word sentence → "... may accumulate irrelevant or noisy content over time. ## 6. Rules with checks (IR1-IR15)

Each rule has a check that can be done by reading or grepping; `scripts/check_paper.py` automates the greppable parts (`== intro & contributions`).

1. **IR1 Root-cause sentence.** The last sentence of the prior-work paragraph names, in one neutral sentence, the design or assumption that all prior practice shares ("Whether A or B, these X share one design: ..."). *Check*: in each drawback bullet's mechanism clause, at least one noun phrase comes from this sentence.
2. **IR2 Planting table.** Every drawback has a source phrase in the prior-work description (a formula term, a number, a pipeline verb). *Check*: write a "drawback → source phrase" table; a missing row means the detail must be added to the description.
3. **IR3 Standard first.** Before the first "However" of the drawbacks, set the two axes with "Intuitively, effective X should be A (\ie ...) and B (\ie ...)"; the method epithet = these two words; they reappear verbatim at least 3 more times (epithet, component names or benefit sentence, closing sentence). *Check*: grep both words, their count and positions; declare the epithet with a `% epithet:` line so `check_paper.py` counts it.
4. **IR4 Drawback structure.** Mechanism + causal connective (thus / leading to / As a result) + consequence; an amplifying setting must carry a number, a figure or a citation; no evaluative nouns (waste, poor, blindly). *Check*: each bullet has a causal connective; each amplifier has evidence.
5. **IR5 Only fixable drawbacks.** Each drawback maps to one numbered component in the key idea. A P1 difficulty the method cannot fix (e.g. the unreliability of short proxies, when the method is itself a short proxy) may appear only as the reason the default practice is expensive, and the intro must then state the comparison frame (e.g. equal compute) and the RQ that tests it. *Check*: number of drawbacks = number of (i)(ii) in the key idea; every P1 difficulty has a destination in P4 or P5.
6. **IR6 Key idea = negation of the root cause.** The key-idea sentence reuses at least 2 content words of the root-cause sentence and flips them (together → separate; fixed before training → set after training). *Check*: put the two sentences side by side and mark the antonym pairs.
7. **IR7 No concept from nowhere.** Every modifier in the key idea (e.g. "short", "weighted") has an earlier motivation; a component that cannot be derived from the key idea is preceded by a challenge sentence "However, ..." with a citation and introduced with "We therefore / To this end". *Check*: list each method component and point to the sentence it answers.
8. **IR8 One centre.** List the last sentence of every paragraph: each lands on the two axes. Theory is written as support for a component, in the middle of the method paragraph; honest reservations become RQ premises ("Since ..., we investigate whether ..."), never the last sentence of the method paragraph. *Check*: the P4 closer contains no however / unclear / whether.
9. **IR9 Term lock.** One name per concept across abstract, intro and RW. *Check*: grep a synonym table (e.g. cheaper / short / small proxy used for one concept; a problem-side adjective pair next to the epithet; "the component alone" next to its fixed name); a fixed term used in the main text must be introduced in the intro at its first occurrence.
10. **IR10 Introducing prior work.** Name at most one modified object in the intro; describe current practice in your own notation; concede before turning (While effective / shows potential); the closest competitors go into a citation cluster and the exact contrast into RW; present a strength prior work already has (e.g. a shared control run) as the part your method keeps. *Check*: named methods in the intro ≤ 1 (cited sources of practice excepted); the sentence before each "However" concedes or states a strength.
11. **IR11 Figure placement.** Each figure reference sits at the step where the figure is evidence (the cost figure at drawback 1's consequence, the framework figure at the key idea); the caption's first sentence states that step's claim. *Check*: list the role of each sentence that cites a figure.
12. **IR12 Related Works closers.** Each paragraph ends with "While effective, such X ..." in the words of the intro's drawback labels, or with "However, ... remains largely underexplored" (scope narrowed to your contribution); only the last paragraph writes "To address these gaps, we propose ...", recovering every gap with the epithet and the two component names. Never "Our work differs from them", "is not our contribution" (as the closer), "first". *Check*: read each paragraph's last sentence; `check_paper.py` greps the three phrases.
13. **IR13 Length conservation.** When rewriting the intro, the word count by the same counter (citations removed, each formula = 1 word) must not exceed the old draft; compile and check the page end. *Check*: the counter + a build.
14. **IR14 Banned forms.** No sentence-initial "Ideally,", no "we argue", no contribution list, no "Surprisingly,"; the closing sentence lands on the benefit on the two axes, not on the evaluation design. *Check*: grep (`check_paper.py` flags "Ideally,", "we argue" and contribution-list phrasing).
15. **IR15 Claim strength.** Each number in a benefit sentence is attributed to the design that produces it (e.g. a 10× saving that comes from a shorter run, not from the shared component); theoretical conclusions state their premise (small-step gradient descent, first order). *Check*: next to each numeric claim, point to the design or proposition it comes from.

## 7. Worked case: diagnosing a method draft

A diagnosis of one unpublished method draft (5 paragraphs, 32 sentences, about 870 words) shows how each rule fails in practice. The draft proposed a cheaper proxy X for an expensive evaluation, with two components.

| Break point | What happened | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| P2 → P3 | no root-cause sentence (the one sentence naming the shared design had been cut for space and survived only in the Preliminary and one RW paragraph); "However, <current practice> faces two drawbacks" had no concession before it | IR1, IR10 |
| inside P3 | the standard came after each drawback as "Ideally, ... (termed ...)", and the second ideal already spelled out the method, so P4's key idea could only repeat it | IR3, IR14 |
| P3 → P4 | the key idea introduced "short" runs with no motivation, colliding with P1's own claim that cheaper proxies can mislead | IR5, IR7 |
| inside P4 | the second component appeared without a challenge sentence; the theory sentence compared with a baseline never introduced | IR7, IR9 |
| P4 and P5 closers | the method paragraph ended on "leaving it unclear whether <component> brings any benefit"; the intro ended on the evaluation design | IR8, IR14 |
| axes | a problem-side pair ("costly and inflexible") and the epithet ("A and B") were two different pairs; five centres (cost, fidelity, flexibility, theory, honesty statements) competed | IR3, IR8 |
| attribution | "the shared component reduces the per-candidate cost to one-tenth", while the saving came from the shorter run | IR15 |

The rebuilt chain follows §0: importance → reliable decisions are expensive because smaller or shorter proxies mislead, so the default is the full evaluation → current practice in own notation, with the shared part and the fixed setting planted → root cause "Whether full or short, these proxies share one design: ..." → standard "Intuitively, effective <task> should be A (\ie ...) and B (\ie ...)" → two drawbacks, each violating one property → key idea as the negation of the root cause → challenge "However, <the key idea alone> is not equivalent to <the full evaluation>" \cite{} → the second component → cost attributed to the design that produces it → RQs with the theory's equivalence result as a premise → "We highlight that X makes <task> A and B at the cost of <cheap baseline>." The rebuilt text was 35 words shorter than the draft (IR13).


## 8. Wording checked with `ps.py` (author corpus, papers / uses)

- Use: "Intuitively," 3/6 (H4 2); "effective * should" 1/6 (H4, the prototype of IR3); "While important" 2/6; "The key idea is to" 2/6; "However, while" 2/6; "As a result," 3/6; "rather than" 5/6; "typically" 6/6; "together with" 2/6; "Since the" 4/6; "serves as" 2/6; "Extending" 3/6; "inspired by" 6/6; "remains largely underexplored" 2/6; "may hinder" 1/6 (H5's own sentence).
- Avoid: sentence-initial "Ideally," 0 (H5 has only the mid-sentence "ideally breaks"); "neither ... nor" (only in appendix prompts); "keeps ... from" 0; "leaving it unclear" 0; "deviates from" / "differs from" 0 (say "second-order discrepancy" or name the difference); "verdict" 0; "Different from" 2 uses, but "Our work differs from them" is ref_only in the lexicon.
- Field terms outside the corpus may stay when needed ("a proxy for", "at equal compute", "We design experiments" when experiments are not yet run).
