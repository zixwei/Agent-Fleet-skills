# Taste rules

Rules that come from real reviews of finished films, maps and decks. When a case is disputed, pick the option closer to the spirit of the section: larger, calmer, fewer words.

## Work

- Work in batches: show a batch, wait for "yes" before the next one.
- People, conversations and sums in a public showing are fictional; product metrics come only from the source the user named.
- The bar is competition level, including on a phone.

## Color and type

- Palette and theme come from the product's website; light if the site is light.
- A neat grotesque: Manrope or close to it.
- Colored accent stripes on the edge of cards, entries and nodes are banned everywhere. Brand-colored shutter stripes in a transition between slides are different: that is motion, and it is fine in a deck.
- The Unbounded font is banned everywhere.
- Plates, scheme labels and annotations are thin and on a grid; deck headlines are large and bold.

## Motion

- Disney-style animation: curves, anticipation, overshoot, juice.
- Slower and more detailed, with a speed control and a camera pull-back.
- Arrow: first the line, then the arrowhead; one direction only.
- Reading order is left to right in every row.

## Text on the canvas

- Visual storytelling with captions; remove caveats and useless annotations.
- Image large, code small.
- Not a single label the picture is clear without: no hints, no caveats, no calls to action. A QR goes without a caption.
- Every number has a noun and a moment: not "8 cards" but "up to 8 cards with new facts per call".

## Deck

- Large, bold, infographic; it reads from the back row of a big hall.
- Micro-animations and transitions are mandatory, plus a static PDF.
- Procedural graphics combined with raster images, 3D where it fits naturally; build 1–2 trial slides first.
- Every slide is valuable to the viewer, or it is gone.
- Headlines have no period.
- A small button to the related site at the top right; a fullscreen button on the title slide.
- For a pitch: the founder on the cover, stylized the same way as the rest of the deck; the numbers on the last slide together with the QR, so the audience can look at them during Q&A. For other decks, ask.

## Map

- One big, detailed map rather than several small ones.
- A whiteboard, not a document: canvases of image, text in fragments, no hedging.
- Zoom in for details, zoom out for the whole picture; a click opens a floating window.
- A thin weight for annotations.
- Everything in one place and identical; clean out the clutter.

## Process film

- Competition-level motion design and video settings.
- From a reference take only the animation; draw the real process.
- Slow autoplay by default (0.5×).
- Publish and share the link once the user has agreed on the address.
